# Personas

> These are **proto-personas**: assumptions written down so they can be tested, not the result of user research. After the Phase 3 sessions, update each one with what real users said and mark which assumptions held.

---

## 1. Riya, the budget-first student

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age / role** | 20, third-year engineering student, Delhi |
| **Trip** | 3 days in Rishikesh or Goa with 3–4 friends, by train or bus |
| **Budget** | Tight and fixed: about ₹5,000–8,000 per person, all-in **[assumption]** |
| **Plans on** | Phone, in between classes; shares plans on WhatsApp |
| **Tools today** | MakeMyTrip or IRCTC, Google Maps, Instagram reels, ChatGPT |

**Goal:** a plan the whole group agrees on that doesn't go over budget.

**Frustrations**
- Adds up costs by hand in a notes app and still gets surprised by extra costs.
- ChatGPT plans sound great but list prices she can't trust.
- The group argues over the plan because nobody can see the full picture.

**What she needs from WanderAI**
- A per-person total cost, shown up front.
- A day-by-day plan she can share (PDF or a link).
- Cheap options first: hostels, local food, free activities.

> "I don't need the best hotel. I need to know we can afford the whole trip."

## 2. Arjun, the time-poor professional

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age / role** | 26, software engineer, Bengaluru |
| **Trip** | Weekend getaway (Coorg, Pondicherry), Friday night to Sunday, with partner or solo |
| **Budget** | Flexible, about ₹15,000–25,000 total **[assumption]** |
| **Plans on** | Laptop, Thursday evening, in under 30 minutes |
| **Tools today** | Google Travel, Booking.com, Google Maps saved lists |

**Goal:** a good, low-effort weekend without spending an evening researching.

**Frustrations**
- Too many options; comparing hotels across sites eats the evening.
- Generic "top 10 things to do" lists ignore that he only has 1.5 days.
- Forgets travel time between places and over-packs the schedule.

**What he needs from WanderAI**
- A realistic schedule that fits the time he actually has.
- 2–3 strong options, not 20.
- Booking links so he can act right away.

> "Just give me a good plan for Saturday and Sunday. I'll book it tonight."

## 3. Meera, the interest-led explorer (secondary)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age / role** | 23, master's student or first job, Pune |
| **Trip** | 4–5 days solo in Jaipur or Hampi, built around one interest (food, history, photography) |
| **Budget** | Moderate; will pay more for the right experience |
| **Plans on** | Laptop, a week or two ahead |
| **Tools today** | Blogs, YouTube, ChatGPT, Google Maps |

**Goal:** a trip shaped around what she loves, not a tourist checklist.

**Frustrations**
- Generic plans suggest the same five tourist spots.
- Has to dig through blogs to find places that match her interest.

**What she needs from WanderAI**
- Plans that start from her interests and fit the budget around them.
- Short reasons for why each stop is there.

> "I'd rather see three great food streets than ten monuments."

**Why she matters:** Riya (budget-first) and Meera (interest-first) are the two planning styles in the Phase 3 A/B test.

---

## Primary persona for v1

**Riya.** She has the sharpest pain (a hard budget), she's the easiest to recruit for testing (classmates), and the budget check is the feature that sets WanderAI apart from plain ChatGPT. Arjun is the second target. Meera is mainly there to shape the A/B test.

## Assumptions to check in user sessions

| # | Assumption | Persona | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Budget total is the most valued feature | Riya | Ask testers to rank features |
| 2 | Users share plans in groups | Riya | Ask "who did you send it to?" |
| 3 | Fewer, better options beat more options | Arjun | Watch whether testers ask for more |
| 4 | Interest-first plans feel more personal | Meera | A/B test satisfaction score |
